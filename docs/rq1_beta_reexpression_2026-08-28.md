`docs/decisions/rq1-beta-reexpression.json` (complete) and

## 1. Metric and conventions

Per branch, per conditioning level h,

    beta(h) = -Phi^-1( P_f(h) ),   dbeta(h) = beta_transient - beta_static

- beta is strictly decreasing in P_f, so an interval on P_f maps to one
  on beta by swapping its endpoints. The exact Clopper-Pearson intervals
  already persisted with every sweep are reused unchanged; no new
  statistical machinery is introduced.
- P_f above 0.5 gives a negative beta and is reported as such.
- A zero-failure level gives an infinite point beta. The deliverable
  there is a one-sided bound: the Clopper-Pearson **upper** bound on
  P_transient gives a **lower** bound on beta_transient, and the
  **lower** bound on P_static gives an **upper** bound on beta_static,
  so their difference is a lower bound on dbeta. This is the exact
  analogue of the ratio bound already used at KP 57.4.
- dbeta intervals are paired bootstrap over the shared realization set,
  B = 1000 replicates, percentile method, one row
  resample per replicate applied to both branches. Reported only where
  both branches carry at least 30 failing rows
  (the R1 floor); below that the bound above is the deliverable.
- The resolution criteria R1 (at least 30 transient failing rows) and R2
  (interval width factor at most 2) remain defined on the probability
  ratio B exactly as pre-registered. They are not restated on dbeta:
  the map is monotone, so a level resolved on B is resolved on dbeta.

Reading, stated in both directions as the campaign requires:

- **B decays and dbeta does not.** Over the attainable range of the four matrix strata the ratio falls by a factor of 29 to 269 and lands between 1.04 and 1.43, that is, within a few tens of percent of parity. Over the same range dbeta is shallowly U-shaped: it dips by at most 0.14 on the low shoulder and then rises monotonically to its maximum at the top attainable stage, a total rise of up to 0.76.
- So the claim that the two models converge toward parity at extreme overload is a statement about P_f and about the failure tail only. It is false of the survival tail, which is where a ratio of failure probabilities stops carrying information and beta keeps carrying it.
- The existing claim that conventional practice is most conservative exactly at design levels is a ratio-space statement. It survives re-scoped to the ratio; in dbeta the design level is near the flat part of the curve, not its maximum.

## 4. The comparator ladder as additive dbeta steps

beta is a per-branch scalar, so the engine ladder telescopes exactly:

    dbeta_total = [beta(C1)-beta(C0)] + [beta(C3b)-beta(C1)]
                + [beta(C4b)-beta(C3b)]

with C0 the production static branch, C1 the crack-reduced static
comparator, C3b the analytic sustained-peak limit and C4b the
production transient branch. The three steps are the head convention,
the initiation gate and the temporal mechanism. No share-of-total
denominator is needed, and the components add rather than multiply.

`n/a` marks a step whose comparator has no failing realization at that stage, so its beta is infinite and the step carries no finite value; the total is then a one-sided bound. `resolved` is the pre-registered R1-and-R2 verdict on the ratio B at that stage, carried over unchanged.

Paired-bootstrap intervals on every step are in the JSON companion
under `ladder.<section>.by_n.<N>.stages[].steps.<component>.ci`.

Two things the additive form makes visible that the share-of-gap form
did not:

- **The initiation gate contributes exactly zero at KP 62.0** at every named stage (C1 and C3b have identical failure sets there), so the section's whole gap is head convention plus temporal.
- **The two surviving components move in opposite directions with stage.** At KP 62.0 between 46.39 and 50.50 m MSL the head-convention step falls from 0.36 to 0.10 while the temporal step rises from 0.55 to 1.56. The near-flat total is the sum of a decaying head-convention term and a growing temporal one, not a flat mechanism.

The `k_aq_field_geomean` arm has no failing realization on either branch in 10^6, so nothing about it is quotable in either metric. The `k_aq_field_toe`, `z_toe_plus0.30m` and `L_withdrawn_1998` arms carry fewer than the 30 transient rows the pre-registered R1 floor requires, so they are one-sided bounds and their B values are likewise unresolved.

Across the arms that clear the R1 floor the bracket spans B = 2.59 to 27.2, a factor of 10.5. In dbeta the same arms span 0.86 to 0.97, a range of 0.11. Taking every arm's point estimate, including the three whose transient counts are below the floor, the spread is B = 2.59 to 38.0, a factor of 15, against dbeta 0.76 to 0.97, a range of 0.21.

The re-expression does not make the bracket go away, and it should not be reported as if it did. What it does is change the bracket's apparent size relative to the number it brackets: an order of magnitude in B becomes about two tenths of a beta unit, which is roughly 2 times the width of the anchor's own 95 percent sampling interval [0.85, 0.97]. In ratio terms the same comparison reads as a factor-of-ten disagreement; in index terms it reads as a two tenths shift on a difference of about nine tenths. Both statements are true of the same eight populations.

## 6. Canonical-event exposure in dbeta

The static branch is exactly invariant between the two canonical
members, so the whole displacement sits in the transient branch.

| Section | level [m MSL] | P_transient production | P_transient alternate | dbeta production | dbeta alternate | shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KP 58.8 | 41.00 | 0.26273 | 0.14828 | 1.22 | 1.63 | +0.41 |
| KP 60.0 | 42.75 | 0.31427 | 0.15210 | 1.87 | 2.41 | +0.54 |

Not quoted, because the alternate member leaves too few failing transient realizations at N = 10^5 to clear the R1 floor: KP 57.4 (0 rows), KP 62.0 (4 rows).
The direction is the same at every section that resolves: the shorter, flashier alternate member lowers the transient probability, raises beta_transient, and therefore WIDENS the index difference. The canonical event is an exposure on this comparison in the conservative direction, that is, the production member is the one that flatters the transient model.
